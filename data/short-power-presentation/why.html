The sextic has a dense expansion but a short power presentation:

F=\sum_{i=0}^{2}(X_i^6+Y_i^6)+\frac{5}{7^7}\cdot\bigl((L+M)^6+(L-M)^6\bigr),

where L=X_0+2\cdot X_1+3\cdot X_2 and M=Y_0+3\cdot Y_1+5\cdot Y_2. The global involution exchanges the last two powers. The middle catalecticant has rank eight, so the presentation cannot be reduced to a six-power Fermat form by a linear change of coordinates.

Over \mathbb C, the eight power coordinates realize the fourfold as a codimension-two linear section of the Fermat sextic sixfold in \mathbb P^7. This supplies a small configuration of linear forms, an explicit diagonal ambient variety, and a controlled family of linear sections. Vanishing cycles of those sections and correspondences arising from diagonal covers are concrete objects to investigate. The coefficient expansion contains all 236 monomials allowed by the involution, but the eight-form presentation retains information that an arbitrary dense equation lacks.

The ambient geometry does not itself supply the required support. The section has new primitive middle cohomology; restricting ambient cohomology does not exhaust it. Nor do the eight forms separate into independent blocks. The scalar Hessian symmetrizer rules out a nontrivial linear direct-sum presentation. The quadratic apolar base locus recovers the eight power points, and their incidence and coefficients leave only the global involution as a projective linear symmetry; there is no hidden reflection quotient here.

The target A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3 asks whether the short power presentation can nevertheless be converted into a serving correspondence or certificate. Its potential advantage is the explicit diagonal configuration, not an existing all-target theorem for this fourfold. A construction that handles this section's primitive part would extend the diagonal methods beyond merely rewriting a split equation.
