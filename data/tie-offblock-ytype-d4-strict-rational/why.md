The sextic is Fermat plus the single tie \(X_0^2\cdot X_1^2\cdot Y_1^2\). The requested monomial is
\[
A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^2\cdot Y_1.
\]
The square \(Y_0^2\) lies outside the coupled ternary block. This is the central feature of the example: the target is neither confined to that block nor an arbitrary six-variable expression.

Relabeling \(Y_0,Y_1\) turns the equation into the familiar single-tie sextic, but transforms the target into \(X_0^3\cdot Y_0\cdot Y_1^2\). That is a different target direction in the same character as the central cubic \(X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3\). Recognizing the same hypersurface is consequently not enough to recycle a certificate; the requested cohomology class must be tracked through the coordinate change as well.

The Hesse cubic in the active squares supplies candidate contact geometry. The passive square raises a separate question: can it multiply an active construction without altering the required divergence? The answer depends on which variables the field actually differentiates, rather than simply on which variables appear somewhere in \(F\). This makes the entry useful for distinguishing geometric structure from a superficial pattern in the expanded polynomial.
