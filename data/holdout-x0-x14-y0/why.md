This holdout target is
\[
A=X_0\cdot X_1^4\cdot Y_0.
\]
It places four degrees in the middle even coordinate and uses the initial pair only through \(X_0\cdot Y_0\). The shape resembles binary and ternary contact constructions with a polynomial multiplier. Here the multiplier is part of the coupled geometry, rather than an independent passive variable.

Indeed, \(X_1\) appears to the first power in the initial coupling and to the third power in the endpoint coupling. A field moving in the initial pair encounters those coefficients; a field moving in the middle pair sees both ends. The derivative and divergence identities must account for these interactions together. The numerator's three-variable support does not reduce the problem to a three-variable equation.

This target belongs to the four-dimensional character component conjugate to the six containing the central cubic, among the ten paired-sign directions missed by the direct quotient-support span. In particular, the endpoint reflection fixes its numerator but reverses the residue volume. These are limitations of the direct quotient methods, not obstructions to every possible support.

The [central holdout construction](#case/holdout) replaces the direct quotient by a Fermat cover and a moving-conic family. Its primitive theorem accepts polynomial sources on the weighted quotient. This offers a concrete route for incorporating the middle even-coordinate multiplier while retaining all the couplings in the equation.

The unresolved specialization is a density calculation: identify a source for the prescribed monomial, check its normal derivative, and show that trace removes the reflected correction. Treating \(X_1\) as constant in an isolated integration would not justify the physical divergence identity.
