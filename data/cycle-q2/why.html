Use the cyclic three-link sextic from the degree-six cyclic instance, with

q=2,\qquad A=X_0^2\cdot X_1^2\cdot X_2^3\cdot Y_0^2\cdot Y_1^2\cdot Y_2.

Every coordinate occurs, and the unequal exponents in the final pair distinguish one position in the cycle. Cyclic permutation produces two companion targets on the same hypersurface. A construction for their invariant sum must still separate the other components to serve the specified target.

The pole order requires field components of degree seven and divisibility of A-2dF(v)+F\operatorname{div}(v) by F^3. Multiplying a degree-six certificate by a polynomial contributes derivatives of that multiplier and does not generally give this identity. The cyclic Jacobian construction establishes a different, second-order-pole identity.

There is also a representation-theoretic distinction. The sextic is invariant under the sixth-root diagonal action with weights (2,1,0,3,2,1). The present numerator has weight seventeen, while the projective volume contributes nine, so the residue has character \zeta_6^{17+9}=\zeta_6^2. Averaging to the invariant quotient therefore erases it. The paired-sign sector theorem supports the invariant sector; its conclusion does not cover this nontrivial character. This limits that quotient construction, rather than establishing nonexistence of a certificate.

The closed cycle also differs from the holdout q=2 target. The chain's all-target support theorem applies at every pole order, but its auxiliary exchange is lost when the closing coupling produces an unmatched term on the cover. The complementary diagonal quotient likewise does not preserve that coupling. Thus the problem tests both which representation the supporting geometry reaches and how its primitive is transported at the higher pole order, while retaining a sparse equation and explicit symmetries.
