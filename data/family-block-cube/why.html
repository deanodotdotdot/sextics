The active binary block is p(u,w)=u^3+u^2\cdot w+2\cdot u\cdot w^2+3\cdot w^3, with (u,w)=(X_0^2,Y_0^2). The target is the balanced monomial X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3. In the diagonal limit, block homogeneity makes this target especially accessible; the mixed cubic coefficients test how that advantage changes under deformation.

The parameter \theta=0 refers to a point used to compare the cubic with an osculating cube. Matching a cubic and a cube to first order supplies contact information, but does not automatically give a certificate. The remaining spectator terms must also be compatible with the divergence condition. This example is useful precisely because it distinguishes a promising contact shape from the additional conditions that make that shape serve the target.

The equation includes a non-diagonal binary sextic in X_1,X_2. Keeping that term tests whether the construction depends on four separate spectator sixth powers or merely on leaving certain variables undifferentiated. It also helps distinguish structural freedom from coefficients that only appear arbitrary in an incomplete formula.

Patterns in earlier coefficient solutions suggest a block-cube family, with compatibility conditions on the positions of the spectator contributions in an auxiliary pencil. This fixed fiber provides a manageable setting in which to study that compatibility. It is not a request to solve the same monomial with every possible cube or every possible placement of spectator terms.
