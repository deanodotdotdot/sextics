The equation separates into two copies of the ternary sextic
\[
f(z_0,z_1,z_2)=z_0^6+(z_1-z_2)\cdot(z_1^5+z_2^5).
\]
Its binary part has a visible linear factor, while the remaining coordinate contributes a sixth power. This makes it a particularly transparent setting for studying how a special presentation of \(F\) can identify a useful primitive target.

The target is
\[
A=-10\cdot X_0\cdot Y_0\cdot(X_1^4+X_2^4).
\]
The single \(Y_0\) factor supplies the required odd parity and can act as a passive multiplier for geometry confined to the \(X\) block.

The relevant broader presentation is \(f=c\cdot e^3+P\cdot k\), where \(e\) is quadratic and the degrees of \(P,k\) add to six. The displayed fiber chooses \(e=z_0^2\) and \(P=z_1-z_2\). This links it to the perfect-power contact constructions in [Chapter 2](#technical-information/explicit-constructions), while making the role of the contact form unusually easy to see.

Along the line \(P=0\), the restriction is a perfect cube of the quadratic \(e\). Thus the natural auxiliary geometry includes a cubic cover, complementing the double-cover picture supplied by a square contact.

The mathematical question is what information that presentation supplies about a target and how robust the information is under changes to \(P\), \(k\), or the passive block. The entry is a representative of that construction family; it does not impose a hyperplane denominator as part of the challenge or assume that one was the original objective. Its value is the chance to compare the same mechanism across different contact degrees. [Contact cone 46006](#case/contact46006) provides the cubic-contact comparison.
