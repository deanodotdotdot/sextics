This is the sextic of [One reflection · residual sector](#case/one-reflection), with a target odd in \(Y_2\) and symmetric in \(Y_0,Y_1\). The \(Y_2\) reflection predicts existence by [Chapter 3, §3.3](#technical-information/geometric-support/33-reflection-quotients). The companion target's explicit binary-cubic construction, however, uses the densities \(Y_0^3+Y_1^3\) and \(Y_0^3-Y_1^3\); the transverse target lies outside both density sectors, and passive multipliers do not remove that obstruction.

This separates a geometric existence statement from the image of a particular explicit construction. In the reflection quotient \(u=Y_2^2\), the Jacobian density is \(2Y_2\). The prescribed odd target has the right density character, but a primitive for it still has to be constructed on the quotient and transported with the correct derivatives and denominator.

The [central holdout's finite-cover construction](#case/holdout) provides another concrete comparison. Its Fermat cover creates an exchange and a moving-conic family, whose integration theorem can accept different polynomial sources. Applying that idea here changes the coefficient pattern: both mixed terms use \(Y_0Y_1\), unlike the two distinct odd pairs in the chain equation. The relevant incidence and source identities must therefore be checked on this specialization; a family that works at generic unequal coefficients cannot be assumed valid on an equal-coefficient locus.

A useful solution would make the transverse support effective through either route, or supply another certificate entirely. The target is deliberately kept simple so that the difficulty lies in serving the missing density sector, rather than handling an elaborate numerator. The challenge imposes no denominator-degree bound and does not require an expanded divisor if the existential theorem can be proved by finite algebraic constructions.
