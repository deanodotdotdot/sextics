The broken-reflection construction reduces this sextic to a cubic surface using the quadratic coordinates S=2(X_0^2+Y_0^2), T=4X_0Y_0, and passive ratios. Its Jacobian supplies the factor X_0^2-Y_0^2, which changes sign under X_0\leftrightarrow Y_0. The target served there has precisely that sign. Here the variety is unchanged, but A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3 is invariant under the same exchange. It therefore cannot be obtained as a polynomial density through that particular quotient map. The other visible exchange, X_1\leftrightarrow Y_1, also fixes this target. This isolates a question about which cohomology classes the construction reaches, rather than about finding lines on another cubic surface. A certificate would need a different transfer, a support serving the complementary sector, or a way to combine constructions beyond these quotient densities; the stated obstruction does not exclude such possibilities.

The three paired sign changes provide another reduction: fixing Y_i/X_i makes the equation cubic in the three squares. Paired signs alone, however, are weaker than the hypotheses of the cyclic and transitive Jacobian constructions. Their sixth-root diagonal action with weights (2,1,0,3,2,1) does not preserve this sextic.

Here the difference is geometric. As calculated in Chapter 4, the generic auxiliary elliptic surface obtained from this paired cubic has \chi(\mathcal O)=3 and Kodaira dimension one. It is not uniruled, so one cannot obtain the needed dominant rational-curve family merely by finding another incidence seed while keeping the same passive parameters fixed. The degree-four/six Jacobian surfaces in the cyclic and transitive constructions are a different situation. A transfer serving the complementary character, a construction that varies the passive parameters, or another supporting geometry remains possible; the calculation does not exclude these alternatives.
