The cyclic triple-cover density target uses Z=X_2^3 to reach a weighted Fano quotient. Its explicit free line and nonsingular six-variable incidence provide polynomial primitives on that quotient. This companion retains the exact same sextic and changes only the source to A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3. It asks what can be done outside the cubic density sector.

For X_2\mapsto\zeta_3X_2, the residue volume has character \zeta_3. The served numerator X_0X_2^2Y_0^3 cancels that character; the present numerator is invariant, so its residue is not. In polynomial terms, cubic pullback supplies sources of the form

X_2^2\cdot B(X_0,X_1,X_2^3,Y_0,Y_1,Y_2).

Every monomial in such a source has X_2-exponent congruent to two modulo three. The proposed source has exponent zero. No change of polynomial B repairs this mismatch. Dividing by X_2^2 instead would move the problem outside the proved polynomial-source integration theorem and introduce a nontrivial covering-field character as well as poles; neither is handled by that polynomial-source theorem.

This is a different test from finding another curve on the Fano quotient: its free-line geometry is already available. A solution might construct a primitive with the required nontrivial cyclic character, use branch geometry to transfer support between characters, or replace the quotient by a correspondence that reaches this residue class. The weighted-Fano density theorem alone supplies no existence conclusion for it.

Exact curation checks find no coordinate reflection, no linear direct-sum decomposition, no subset Euler construction, and no serving junior element among the eighteen projective diagonal symmetries. Those are screens of concrete methods, not an exhaustive obstruction. The original smoothness check and saved slice are reused because the hypersurface has not changed. The point of the pair is to isolate target transport from the increasingly effective machinery for constructing and integrating rational-curve families.
