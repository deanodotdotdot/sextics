This is the same two-tie sextic as the second-block contact example: one Hesse cubic uses X_0,X_1,Y_0, and the other uses X_2,Y_1,Y_2. Here, however, the target crosses that decomposition:

A=X_2^3\cdot Y_0\cdot Y_1\cdot Y_2.

The factor Y_0 comes from the first block, while the rest comes from the second.

This change tests a precise aspect of locality. A multiplier from another block can be harmless if it is a first integral of the field, but the same conclusion does not follow merely from the additive decomposition of F. Both the derivative identity and the divergence condition must respect that multiplier. A construction that uses radial motion in all six coordinates may behave differently from one concentrated in an ordered pair inside a Hesse block.

The four odd coordinate exponents give another useful comparison with the two-odd-coordinate target confined to the second block. The plane-cubic coefficients are unchanged, but the target requires a different parity character. A single geometric configuration may therefore have several distinct target images, whose relation is worth understanding before attempting a more general sextic.

A further question is whether the required contact geometry can be used over the rational coefficient field or needs algebraic branches and descent. A smaller denominator, if available, need not arise by cancelling a common factor in a larger one. The instance is useful for comparing the geometric and arithmetic requirements of such presentations.
