A cover can create a reflection that the original equation does not possess. For the central holdout, adjoining T^6=Y_0^6+Y_2^6 and writing Y_0=\alpha T, Y_2=\beta T, y=Y_1 makes the equation symmetric under y\leftrightarrow T. The fixed coefficient curve \alpha^6+\beta^6=1 and a moving conic on the quotient then support the cover-and-trace construction.

This instance adds just

\frac17 X_0^2\cdot Y_0^2\cdot Y_2^2

to that sextic, keeping the same target A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3. All three paired sign changes survive. The perturbation is separately even in every coordinate: its purpose is to change the branch geometry without introducing another odd chain coupling.

On the original cover the extra term is \frac17 X_0^2\cdot\alpha^2\cdot\beta^2\cdot T^4. Interchanging y,T changes the equation by

\frac17 X_0^2\cdot\alpha^2\cdot\beta^2\cdot(y^4-T^4).

Thus the old quotient coordinates S=y^2+T^2, P=yT no longer describe the complete equation. Absorbing the perturbation into the definition of T instead gives

\alpha^6+\beta^6+\frac17(X_0/T)^2\cdot\alpha^2\cdot\beta^2=1.

The coefficient curve now varies with an active coordinate. The derivative compatibility and normal-source identities proved over the fixed Fermat curve cannot simply be reused.

The complementary diagonal quotient does not avoid this change. Its weights (5,1,5) on Y give the perturbation weight two modulo six, so it no longer preserves the equation. The two chain constructions fail at their geometric input, before a choice of polynomial source or curve seed.

A solution would therefore test whether a cover-and-trace argument can accommodate moving branch data. Possible approaches include a different auxiliary cover, a transport calculation that incorporates the varying coefficient relation, or a new family of rational curves. The deformed-ternary companion demonstrates the different situation in which a replacement conic and its nonsingular incidence suffice because the original cover symmetry and coefficient curve remain available. The shared lifting theorem does not restore a symmetry absent from the changed equation.

Exact checks establish smoothness and rule out linear direct-sum decompositions, coordinate-reflection formulas, and the target-fixing junior diagonal quotients screened in Chapter 4. These are boundaries of specified constructions, not a claim that every possible method has been excluded. No certificate or general existence conclusion for this perturbed target is supplied here.
