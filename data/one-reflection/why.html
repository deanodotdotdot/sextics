The sextic is a sparse deformation of Fermat with two couplings,

X_0^3\cdot X_1\cdot Y_0\cdot Y_1 +X_1^3\cdot X_2\cdot Y_0\cdot Y_1.

Both involve the same pair of odd coordinates. The target is the degree-six monomial X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3, keeping the requested class simple while changing the coupling geometry from the central holdout. The visible reflection in Y_2 alone does not serve this target, since its numerator is even in Y_2.

Two additional reflections become visible after the block-linear change

P=Y_0+Y_1,\qquad M=Y_0-Y_1.

Indeed, Y_0\cdot Y_1=(P^2-M^2)/4, while Y_0^6+Y_1^6 is separately even in P and M. Consequently F is separately even in P,M,Y_2. This coordinate change commutes with the global sign involution, and the target decomposes as

A=\frac18\cdot X_0^3\cdot P\cdot(P^2+3\cdot M^2) +\frac18\cdot X_0^3\cdot M\cdot(M^2+3\cdot P^2).

The two summands are odd under the P and M reflections, respectively.

Thus existence is already known geometrically from the reflection theorem in Chapter 3: each summand descends to the appropriate weighted Fano quotient, whose rational-curve construction provides divisor support. Adding the supported classes and applying the certificate theorem gives existence for this target. This geometric argument supplies a finite construction in principle; turning it into a usable rational field requires additional algebraic work.

The constructive challenge is to turn these hidden reflections into usable equations for a supporting divisor and a rational vector field. The shared factor X_1\cdot Y_0\cdot Y_1 in the perturbation offers an additional algebraic handle. A successful construction would show how changing coordinates can expose useful quotient geometry even when the displayed target lies outside the sector served by the most obvious reflection. Compare the coupled Fano target, where the favorable reflection character is visible from the outset.
