The holdout chain has mixed terms only of X/Y-bidegree (4,2). This example uses chain coefficients 1/2 and adds the opposite biweight

\frac13\cdot X_0\cdot X_2\cdot Y_0^3\cdot Y_2.

The global involution and all three paired sign changes survive. The target is still X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3, making this a comparison of coupling geometry for the same prescribed monomial.

The holdout cover construction shows exactly where its reduction ceases to apply. On T^6=Y_0^6+Y_2^6, set \alpha=Y_0/T, \beta=Y_2/T, and y=Y_1. The new coupling is

\frac13\cdot\alpha^3\cdot\beta\cdot X_0\cdot X_2\cdot T^4.

Without a corresponding y^4 term, the exchange y\leftrightarrow T no longer preserves the equation. The polynomial cannot be passed unchanged to the quotient with coordinates S=y^2+T^2, P=yT. The issue is therefore not merely an inconvenient exponent pattern in the final vector field: the auxiliary reflection itself has disappeared.

The complementary diagonal action with weights (5,1,5) on Y does preserve the chain terms, but multiplies this new term by \zeta^2. Its quotient and polynomial-density construction therefore cannot be substituted for the broken cover without another change of geometry.

A useful approach would either replace that reflection or construct primitives on a cover with the extra fourth-power term retained. The mixed-polar target tests a related asymmetric quartic term with a different placement and target, while broken paired signs changes the finite symmetry without adding a second biweight. These comparisons help separate the actual hypotheses of a proposed extension.

The cyclic cubic construction suggests a more direct comparison that retains the paired signs. Write Y_i=r_iX_i and u_i=X_i^2. The extra term becomes \frac13r_0^3r_2u_0^2u_2, while the two chain terms give u_0^2u_1 and u_1^2u_2. These form a transitive pattern rather than the directed cubic cycle of that construction. Plane-cubic invariant theory can still be applied, but the Jacobian coefficients, nonsingular polynomial-curve family, and physical source transport are different. A primitive on the already solved cyclic Jacobian cannot simply be substituted here.
