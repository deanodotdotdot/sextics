The sextic is invariant under simultaneous permutations of the three \(X/Y\) pairs. A transposition exchanges two \(X\) coordinates and two \(Y\) coordinates at once. Its fixed subspace has codimension two, so it is not a reflection. The equation couples all three pairs and has scalar Hessian symmetrizer, ruling out a linear block decomposition.

The target

\[
A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3-X_1^3\cdot Y_1^3
\]

lies in the two-dimensional standard representation of \(S_3\). Its average over the group is zero. A simultaneous permutation has determinant one on the six coordinates, so the residue volume introduces no extra sign: this is the representation of the residue itself, not just a statement about its polynomial numerator.

This differs from the [separate-permutation example](#case/symmetric-sextic-residual), whose remaining class has a one-dimensional alternating character in each factor. Here one must handle a genuine two-dimensional component. The invariant quotient projects that component away, and the ordinary reflection theorem does not apply to the simultaneous action. The obvious finite quotients offer no shortcut through the low-age criterion either: the nontrivial fixed strata of \(S_3\) together with the global involution all have age at least one.

The symmetry still gives a concrete organizing principle. Covariants, search spaces, and correspondences can be decomposed into a small list of representations. The fixed loci of transpositions and three-cycles supply explicit branch strata, while the standard component suggests matrix-valued rather than scalar-character transfer. These are possibilities to develop, not certificates already supplied by the group action.

A solution must use the nonabelian structure while retaining the chosen component. The target is designed to expose the gap between an invariant-density argument and an equivariant correspondence that reaches a higher-dimensional representation. It is a structured test of that gap, rather than a fully general dense sextic.
