A paired-sign cubic and its Jacobian residue primitive

Statement and scope

This construction addresses the fixed cycle-three equation and the individual source A=X_0^3Y_0^3, with q=1. The three cyclic couplings all retain coefficient 1/2. The final theorem is the exact protected HodgeComputer.HasCertificate statement in Solution.lean.

The argument proves existence of a legal homogeneous polynomial certificate. It does not export a displayed polynomial witness or assert a particular denominator degree. Its intermediate rational functions lie in the function field of the original sextic. An auxiliary Jacobian supplies a primitive, and explicit coordinate and first-variation identities transfer that primitive back to the original source.

The paired-sign cubic

Write p=(X_0,X_1,X_2,Y_0,Y_1,Y_2) for the images of the six coordinates in the sextic function field. Set

R=p_2^2,\qquad v=p_3/p_0,\qquad c_1=(p_4/p_1)/v,\qquad c_2=(p_5/p_2)/v, \qquad x=p_0^2/R,\qquad y=p_1^2/R.

Then the sextic equation is R^3U(x,y,1)=0, where

U(x,y,z)=ax^3+by^3+cz^3+dx^2y+ey^2z+fz^2x

and

\begin{aligned} a&=1+v^6,& b&=1+c_1^6v^6,& c&=1+c_2^6v^6,\\ d&=v^2c_1/2,& e&=v^2c_1c_2/2,& f&=v^2c_2/2. \end{aligned}

PhysicalField.lean proves irreducibility of the actual sextic using a monic Eisenstein specialization. Consequently its quotient is a domain. PhysicalCoordinates.lean and PhysicalChart.lean establish the coordinate identities and nonvanishing needed to use this chart. These conditions are proved in the function field; they are not extra hypotheses on the final theorem.

Covariants and their exact identities

The integral covariants H,\Theta,J are specified in CubicCovariants.lean. Their normalization is fixed throughout. Define

\begin{aligned} s_4&=ae^2f+bdf^2+cd^2e,\\ s_6&=27a^2b^2c^2+4a^2ce^3+4ab^2f^3+6abcdef +4bc^2d^3-d^2e^2f^2. \end{aligned}

On U=0, the covariant identity is

J^2=4\Theta^3+4s_4\Theta H^4-s_6H^6.

Thus

\xi=\Theta/H^2,\qquad \eta=-J/(2H^3) \quad\text{satisfy}\quad \eta^2=\xi^3+s_4\xi-s_6/4.

The proof establishes the full polynomial syzygy, including the term divisible by U, in CubicSyzygy.lean. In particular, the specialization to the physical field is not a numerical check at a selected cubic.

The large polynomial identity is verified by an exact encoding argument. BivariateExpressions.lean proves the semantics of the polynomial operations used to form its 190 coefficients. Each coefficient has a fixed diagonal weight in the six parameters. At a fixed weight, the exponents of a,b,c are determined by those of d,e,f; the latter are less than 19. CubicKronecker.lean therefore proves injectivity of their encoding with exponents 1,19,361. Signed expressions are split into two polynomials with natural coefficients. A proved coefficient bound makes evaluation at 2^{42} injective on these polynomials. PolynomialDigitCheck.lean proves this last step. The coefficient files use kernel reduction to check the resulting finite equalities and bounds. The syntax, grading, products, and evaluation bindings are all proved in Lean; generated data are not trusted as an external oracle.

Let g=U_x. The physical derivation D_y fixes R,v,c_1,c_2, sends y to 1, and sends x to -U_y/g. The covariant differential identity gives

\frac{D_y\xi}{2\eta}=\frac3g.

See PhysicalCubicTangent.lean and CubicFieldDifferential.lean. The nonvanishing of g,H,J, and hence of \eta and D_y\xi, is proved separately.

A polynomial primitive on the auxiliary Jacobian

Put

T=Rv^6,\qquad X=R^2\xi,\qquad Y=R^3\eta.

The homogeneous coefficient polynomials a8 and b256 in JacobianModel.lean give the equation

V=Y^2-X^3-\frac18 a_8(c_1,c_2,1,R,T)X -\frac1{256}b_{256}(c_1,c_2,1,R,T)=0.

Their physical values are 8R^4s_4 and -64R^6s_6, respectively.

The key existence result on this model is stronger than the one source needed here: every polynomial source has a rational residue primitive. JacobianResidue.lean proves this via a polynomial family, polynomial integration, and normalized finite trace.

The family has coordinate degrees (2,4,6) in (R,X,Y), with parameter T, and independent endpoint parameters R(0),X(0). Its thirteen unknown coefficients satisfy thirteen integral coefficient equations. A nonsingular seed over \mathbb F_5, at (c_1,c_2,R(0),X(0))=(2,3,1,3), is

\begin{aligned} R(T)&=1+2T+4T^2,\\ X(T)&=3+T+3T^2+4T^3+4T^4,\\ Y(T)&=2+3T+3T^2+4T^3+4T^4+T^5+T^6. \end{aligned}

JacobianSeedBinding.lean binds the displayed matrix and its checked inverse to the actual derivatives of the incidence equations. The shared arithmetic lifting theorem then supplies a nonsingular generic solution over a finite separable extension of \mathbb C(c_1,c_2,R(0),X(0)). This characteristic-changing step uses the integral square-system theorem, not an inference that a single modular point automatically lifts to characteristic zero.

The coefficient Jacobian also constructs a polynomial normal whose pairing with the hypersurface gradient is 1. The retained endpoint parameters give the required independence and finite function-field extension. Polynomial integration constructs the primitive on the family; normalized finite trace returns it to the Jacobian function field. These steps are developed in JacobianFamily.lean, JacobianDimension.lean, and JacobianNormal.lean.

First variation at the fixed coefficient

For the calculation only, replace a=1+v^6 by a=\alpha+v^6, differentiate, and evaluate at \alpha=1. A dot denotes this first variation. This procedure leaves the submitted equation unchanged.

It is important that differentiation is not postulated as a derivation of the already specialized function field. Instead, CubicJetCalculus.lean uses the dual-number ring K[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2), with

a_\epsilon=a+\epsilon,\qquad x_\epsilon=x-\epsilon x^3/g,

and the other cubic coefficients and y fixed. It proves the cubic equation and covariant identities in this ring. Their two components give the ordinary identity and its first variation.

Writing a_W=s_4, b_W=-s_6/4, this yields the exact source relation

\frac{\dot g}{g} =\frac{\dot a_W\xi+\dot b_W}{2\eta^2} -\frac g3D_y\left(\frac{\dot\xi}{2\eta}\right), \qquad \dot g=3x^2-\frac{x^3U_{xx}}g.

PhysicalCubicJets.lean proves this relation for the actual physical map. The auxiliary polynomial source is P_J=-V_\alpha/(144R), whose explicit polynomial form is in JacobianSource.lean. Its image in the physical field is

P_J=\frac{R^5}{144}(\dot a_W\xi+\dot b_W).

Transport to the original residue equation

Write F_i=\partial_iF, with its image in the function field understood. The physical tangent derivations are \delta_i=\partial_{i+1}-(F_{i+1}/F_0)\partial_0, for 0\leq i<5. For a vector w, the residue divergence with normal F_0 is

\operatorname{div}_{F_0}(w) =\sum_i\delta_iw_i-\frac{\sum_iw_i\delta_iF_0}{F_0}.

The matrix M_{ji}=\delta_i(T,c_1,c_2,R,X)_j is invertible. PhysicalJacobianCoordinates.lean proves its determinant and the exact residue-volume multiplier

\frac{F_0\det M}{2Y}=-144Rv^3.

The inverse matrix supplies coordinate derivations. A characteristic-zero degree-descent argument in CoordinateIndependence.lean proves independence of the five passive coordinates. The monic irreducible Jacobian equation then gives the function-field embedding. PhysicalJacobianMap.lean proves that this embedding intertwines the tangent derivations. Piola transport of the Jacobian primitive therefore has physical residue divergence

-v^3\frac{\dot a_W\xi+\dot b_W}{2\eta^2}.

Set h=p_0F_0+p_3F_3, h_1=p_1F_1+p_4F_4, and \lambda=p_3^3/(4p_0^5p_1^2p_2^2). The vector

W=(\lambda hp_1,0,-\lambda h_1p_3,\lambda hp_4,0)

has zero residue divergence and acts on functions as v^3gD_y. Consequently adding \dot\xi W/(6\eta) supplies exactly the first-variation correction. PhysicalCubicVolume.lean proves both identities for arbitrary rational functions.

There is also a normal-chart correction C=-p_0^3p_3^4/h, placed in the component for p_3. It satisfies

\frac{A_0}{F_0}-\frac{AF_{00}}{F_0^2} =v^3\frac{\dot g}{g} +\operatorname{div}_{F_0}(0,0,C,0,0).

Subtracting this vector completes the physical primitive. The full assembly is PhysicalPrimitive.lean.

Clearing and homogeneity

The resulting vector has residue divergence equal to -A_0/F_0+AF_{00}/F_0^2. The shared residue-clearing theorem chooses a common polynomial denominator and lifts the normal and divergence identities from the quotient field. Irreducibility ensures the denominator can be chosen relatively prime to F. The shared homogeneous-certificate theorem then extracts the required homogeneous data: a nonzero denominator D, six numerators of degree \deg D+1, and a polynomial quotient for the cleared defect. This permits a nonzero quotient, as required by the formal contract.

PhysicalClearing.lean carries out that final step. The construction uses classical existence and finite trace, so no explicit expanded D,N,Q is claimed or supplied. All nonvanishing, relative-primality, degree, and divisibility obligations are part of the resulting certificate.
