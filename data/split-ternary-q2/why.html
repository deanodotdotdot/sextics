Here the two ternary sextics f and g are individually asymmetric, but the equation retains the exact decomposition F=f(X)+g(Y). The target is deliberately a q=2 instance: A=X_0^4\cdot X_1^3\cdot Y_0^3\cdot Y_1^2 has degree twelve and block bidegree (7,5). Its rational differential form has a third-order pole along F=0.

Geometric existence is known, by the split theorem and elliptic contraction of Chapter 3A, §§3A.1–3A.3. The two triple planes u^3=f(X) and v^3=g(Y) acquire divisor support for the required characters after taking products with a Fermat elliptic curve. Their Fano companions supply covering curves; contraction along a translated elliptic diagonal then supports the invariant cohomology of the fourfold. The theorem covers every allowed target and every positive pole parameter, including this exact q=2 target.

The crossed-ternary construction gives an algebraic model for the contraction step: companion primitives, generic elliptic translation, finite residues with an infinity correction, and derivative-compatible descent. That fixed pole-one application does not itself prove this target. The construction by fqlx supplies the companion data for these two ternaries and a second-normal-residue argument for the third-order pole. Its polynomial thickening includes the numerator and Jacobian jets and their tangent correction; weighted descent, elliptic contraction, and physical transport then retain the full strict identity. This makes the instance a worked higher-pole realization of the geometric existence mechanism.

The elementary split Euler field does not settle this choice. For a three-variable block, its strict formula requires target degree three in that block, independently of q; the chosen degrees are seven and five. Neither ternary has a coordinate reflection. Increasing the pole order is therefore a substantive part of this test, rather than multiplication of a known degree-six target by F.

For a denominator of degree d, the six numerators have degree d+7. The certificate establishes the full divisibility by F^3, including lower-pole terms. Identifying only the leading Jacobian-ring component would not suffice. The normalized systems in Chapter 5 provide a concrete computational interface once a promising support is found.
