Polynomial companions and higher residues for split ternaries

Exact target

The construction treats exactly the polynomial and numerator in instance.rec. Put c=5/7, and write

\begin{aligned} f(x,a,y)&=x^6+a^6+y^6+c(x^5a+x^2a^3y+3xy^5+2a^5y),\\ g(r,z,w)&=r^6+z^6+w^6+c(2r^5z+2r^3zw^2+rw^5+3z^5w). \end{aligned}

Thus F=f(X_0,X_1,X_2)+g(Y_0,Y_1,Y_2), q=2, and A=X_0^4X_1^3Y_0^3Y_1^2. On the chart used below, set p=f(x,a,1), q_0=g(r,z,1), and s=x^4a^3r^3z^2. The two block degrees are seven and five; the construction retains this distinction throughout.

Polynomial companion curves

The geometric input consists of the two actual weighted companion cones

7\bigl(f(x,a,y)+S^3+T^3\bigr)=0, \qquad 7\bigl(g(r,z,w)+S^3+T^3\bigr)=0.

The ordinary variables have weight one and S,T have weight two. Both companions reuse a moving-conic seed modulo five, where their asymmetric perturbations vanish. The selected thirteen coefficient equations have Jacobian determinant 1 in \mathbf F_5; the twelve-dimensional free/normal block has determinant 4. These are exact finite matrix calculations. Generic incidence and specialization then give finite extensions of the rational parameter field, polynomial curves on each exact characteristic-zero companion, polynomial normal vectors, and a nonzero evaluation determinant. The finite-field seed supplies these hypotheses through the maintained algebraic machinery; it is not itself the characteristic-zero certificate. See CompanionSeeds.lean, CompanionGeometry.lean, and CompanionResidue.lean.

Second normal residue

The new higher-pole step is a polynomial-family residue construction for a third-order pole. For a hypersurface equation H, a normal coordinate, and g_0=\partial_0H, its second normal source is

R_2(B)=\frac12\left( \frac{B_{00}}{g_0^2} -\frac{3B_0H_{00}+BH_{000}}{g_0^3} +\frac{3BH_{00}^2}{g_0^4}\right).

Choose a polynomial normal vector \psi satisfying dH(\psi)=1, and thicken the curve linearly by c(t)+\varepsilon\psi(t). Write the equation as \varepsilon+a\varepsilon^2+b\varepsilon^3+\cdots, and write the numerator times its Jacobian as K_0+K_1\varepsilon+K_2\varepsilon^2+\cdots. The relevant polynomial integrand is

P=K_2-3aK_1+(6a^2-3b)K_0.

Actual polynomial gradient, Hessian, third derivative, and determinant identities prove

P=J\bigl(R_2(B)+\operatorname{ResDiv}_{g_0}Z\bigr),

with an explicit tangent correction Z. Consequently polynomial integration in the curve parameter, followed by Piola transport, supplies a residue primitive. Derivative-compatible normalized trace descends it from the finite curve field to the companion field. This includes all second-order correction terms. The helper chain is HigherResidue.lean, NormalTaylor.lean, NormalJetBinding.lean, SourceDensityAlgebra.lean, JacobianJets.lean, NormalRowJets.lean, and HigherCurve.lean.

For the first block, use T as the normal coordinate, with h=x^4a^3. On the cleared companion H=7(f+S^3+T^3), take B=49hST. Since g_0=21T^2, the rational first-order source

B'=\frac12\partial_T(B/g_0)=-\frac{7hS}{6T^2}

has exactly R_1(B')=R_2(B), where R_1(C)=\partial_0(C/g_0). The resulting tangent equation has right side -2hS/(9T^5). The function T is proved nonzero in the companion field, so these divisions are legal. CompanionPoleTwo.lean, CompanionPoleTwoFamily.lean, and RationalAmbient.lean bind this conversion to the actual cone.

Radial descent and elliptic contraction

Radial descent uses the map (T,x,a,y,S)=(\rho^2T_0,\rho x_0,\rho a_0,\rho,\rho^2S_0). The independent cone weights are (1,1,1,2); the normal 21T^2 has weight four. The source above has weight minus one, and becomes an affine coefficient divided by \rho. Taking the radial residue therefore gives the required affine primitive. The second companion uses its degree-five polynomial source and the ordinary companion construction. These particular weights, rather than an unrestricted assertion about affine polynomial sources, justify both descents. See CompanionTheta.lean, CompanionThetaBridge.lean, and CompanionPrimitives.lean.

Adjoin u^3=p, v^3=q_0, and use the Fermat elliptic curve a_e^3+b_e^3+1=0, with \delta a_e=b_e^2, \delta b_e=-a_e^2. Scalar conversion of the first companion gives -h a_e/(3u^5b_e^2). The exact identity

\delta(a_e^2/b_e+b_e^2/a_e)=b_e/a_e^2-a_e/b_e^2

converts it, including its base-divergence terms, into the theta source (h/u^5)(b_e/a_e^2). The second companion gives the ordinary source r^3z^2/v^4. Translated elliptic contraction, with its infinity correction, combines these into a divergence primitive for their product. The checked scalar and product bindings are EllipticSource.lean, CompanionScalar.lean, CompanionThetaScalar.lean, CompanionElliptic.lean, CompanionAssembly.lean, and ProductDivergence.lean.

Multiplication by u^5v^4, then normalized trace, produces a rational four-vector W satisfying

\operatorname{div}W-\frac{5W(p)}{3p}-\frac{4W(q_0)}{3q_0}=s.

The physical substitution X=t(x,a,1), Y=t\lambda(r,z,1) gives F=t^6(p+\lambda^6q_0). HigherPoleTransport.lean and PhysicalTransport.lean construct the strict pole-two field, retaining the vertical component and the physical Jacobian correction 2V_\lambda/\lambda.

Legal homogeneous certificate

Finally, CertificateClearing.lean tracks common homogeneous fractions simultaneously in the ambient field and on F=0. Their denominators remain nonzero in the hypersurface function field; exact irreducibility of F makes the resulting denominator relatively prime to F. Clearing gives a homogeneous denominator D of degree d, six numerators of degree d+7, and the full strict identity

AD^2-2D\sum_iN_i\partial_iF+ F\left(D\sum_i\partial_iN_i-\sum_iN_i\partial_iD\right)=0.

The quotient polynomial is Q=0. Solution.lean identifies the protected polynomials exactly. This is an existential certificate construction; it does not export an expanded denominator or six expanded witnesses, and makes no claim of repository acceptance.
