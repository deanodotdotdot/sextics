This instance places the cubic target in the middle pair of the holdout chain:

A=X_1^3\cdot Y_1^3.

It resembles the central X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3 target after relabeling, but the equation is not invariant under that relabeling. The middle coordinates participate in both mixed terms, once with X_0,Y_0 and once with X_2,Y_2. A field adapted to one end cannot simply be moved to the middle without acquiring additional derivative terms.

The monomial is invariant under all three paired sign changes and under the endpoint exchange X_2\leftrightarrow Y_2. The latter reverses the residue volume, so its quotient does not serve the class. The broader direct quotient calculation likewise leaves this target among ten independent directions outside its 48-dimensional supported subspace of the 58-dimensional paired-sign sector. The relevant distinction is the character of the residue, including its volume determinant, rather than the numerator alone.

The central holdout construction supplies a more promising form of uniformity. Its auxiliary cover has a moving-conic family whose integration theorem accepts polynomial sources on the quotient. The middle cubic can therefore be approached by seeking a new source on that same geometry, without pretending that the oriented chain is invariant under moving its endpoints.

The source identity must retain both neighboring couplings. Its normal derivative must match the protected target, and the deck trace must remove the reflected contribution rather than alter the class. These are concrete algebraic obligations beyond the existence of a primitive for an unspecified source.

Compare the endpoint cube. The two numerators have the same shape but occupy different positions in the equation. Determining whether one geometric family serves both tests the scope of the construction more sharply than producing a certificate only for a symmetry-invariant sum.
