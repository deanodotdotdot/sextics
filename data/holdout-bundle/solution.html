Complementary diagonal quotient and moving-conic certificate

This construction addresses the unchanged holdout-bundle instance, in the variable order (X0,X1,X2,Y0,Y1,Y2), with pole parameter q=1:

F=\sum_{j=0}^2X_j^6+\sum_{j=0}^2Y_j^6 +X_0^3X_1Y_0Y_1+X_1^3X_2Y_1Y_2, \qquad A_{\rm bundle}=X_0^3Y_0^3+X_0^3Y_0Y_1^2.

The new work concerns A_*=X_0^3Y_0Y_1^2. The accepted Fermat-cover construction supplies the first summand. The certificate is an existence proof over \mathbb C, with unrestricted finite denominator degree and an unrestricted quotient remainder Q. No rational-coefficient or strict-certificate restriction is imposed.

1. The change of geometry

The old cover has deck exponent zero on this source. Its trace-zero antisymmetrization cannot supply the missing summand. Instead use the complementary action

(Y_0,Y_1,Y_2)\longmapsto(\zeta^5Y_0,\zeta Y_1,\zeta^5Y_2).

Its determinant character is \zeta^5, while the polynomial source has character \zeta. Thus the pole-two source including its volume form is invariant.

For the requested initial quotient coordinates p=Y_0Y_1,r=Y_2/Y_0,R=Y_0^6, put S=X_0^6+X_1^6+X_2^6, C=X_0^3X_1+rX_1^3X_2. Then

G=(1+r^6)R^2+(S+Cp)R+p^6, \quad G(\phi)=Y_0^6F, \quad\det(d\phi)=6Y_0^5.

Consequently the downstairs density is B_G=X_0^3p^2R/6, since B_G(\phi)\det(d\phi)/Y_0^{12}=A_*. These identities alone do not construct a primitive.

Fix i^2=-1, and change the fibration birationally by

v=rp=Y_1Y_2, \qquad \lambda=\frac{p^3}{R(1+ir^3)} =\frac{Y_1^3}{Y_0^3+iY_2^3}.

Write a=\lambda+\lambda^{-1} and b=i(\lambda-\lambda^{-1}). The relative hypersurface is

K=X_0^6+X_1^6+X_2^6+X_0^3X_1p+X_1^3X_2v+ap^3+bv^3.

The identities G=RK and \det\partial(p,v,\lambda)/\partial(p,r,R)=-p\lambda/R give the exact density conversion

\frac{B_G\,dp\wedge dr\wedge dR}{G^2} =\frac{\rho\,dp\wedge dv\wedge d\lambda}{K^2}, \qquad \rho=-\frac{X_0^3p}{6\lambda}.

The retained X-volume factors are understood on both sides. For the direct physical map \theta=(X_0,X_1,X_2,p,v,\lambda),

K(\theta)=F,\qquad J_\theta=\det(d\theta)=-6\lambda Y_1,\qquad J_\theta\rho(\theta)=A_*.

This is a fibration over \mathbb C(\lambda). The proof makes no Fano, rationality, or canonical-boundary assertion from the equation's appearance. It constructs and checks the curve correspondence below.

2. Exact incidence through the generic endpoint

Over k=\mathbb C(\lambda), take the generic endpoint field

H=\operatorname{Frac} k[c_0,c_2,p_0,v_0]/(c_0^6+c_2^6+ap_0^3+bv_0^3).

The endpoint polynomial and K are proved irreducible; in particular these coordinate rings are domains. The independent endpoint parameters are c_2,p_0,v_0, and c_0\ne0.

Use twelve unknowns u_0,\ldots,u_{11} and the polynomial curve

\begin{aligned} \Phi_0&=c_0+u_0t+u_1t^2,&\Phi_1&=t,\\ \Phi_2&=c_2+u_2t+u_3t^2,\\ \Phi_p&=p_0+u_4t+u_5t^2+u_6t^3+u_7t^4,\\ \Phi_v&=v_0+u_8t+u_9t^2+u_{10}t^3+u_{11}t^4. \end{aligned}

Its incidence equations are exactly

E_j=[t^j]K(\Phi)=0\quad(1\le j\le12).

The constant equation is the endpoint relation, and all higher coefficients vanish by the degree bound. Every one of the twelve coefficient directions is included in the Jacobian \Delta_{j\ell}=\partial E_j/\partial u_\ell.

There is an exact nonsingular point over \mathbb F_5:

(a,b)=(0,3),\quad(c_0,c_2,p_0,v_0)=(1,1,4,1), \quad u=(1,0,4,1,4,2,2,4,4,1,1,2), \qquad\det\Delta=3.

BundleConicSeed.lean checks all coefficients, all 144 Jacobian entries, and an explicit inverse matrix. The modular calculation is only an input to the characteristic-zero construction:

  1. The endpoint (1,1,4,1) satisfies its relation over the integers at (a,b)=(0,-2). The Jacobian-localized square incidence is nonzero and étale over \mathbb Z; flatness and the complex-point theorem produce an actual complex nonsingular conic.
  2. The integral parameter ring is \mathbb C[\lambda,\lambda^{-1}]. Specialization at \lambda=i gives (a,b)=(0,-2), so that complex conic proves nontriviality of the generic incidence over the endpoint domain.
  3. Étale flatness and injectivity of the endpoint-domain map preserve nontriviality at the generic endpoint. A maximal field factor gives a finite separable extension E/H, its twelve coefficients, K(\Phi)=0, and invertible \Delta.

This is formalized in BundleConicLift.lean and BundleIncidence.lean. Neither a nor \lambda^2+1 is inverted at the seed.

3. Polynomial normal, determinant, and finite correspondence

Put g_j=K_j(\Phi). Begin with the constant normal \Psi^{(0)}=e_0/(6c_0^5). Its pairing with g has constant coefficient one. The inverse incidence Jacobian solves for the twelve positive-coefficient variations that cancel every remaining coefficient. More explicitly, let V_\ell=\partial\Phi/\partial u_\ell, and set \eta_j=[t^j](g\cdot\Psi^{(0)}-1), for 1\le j\le12. Then one may take

\Psi=\Psi^{(0)}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{11}(\Delta^{-1}\eta)_\ell V_\ell.

All pairings have degree at most twelve, and every V_\ell has zero constant term. Thus there is an actual polynomial vector \Psi\in E[t]^5 with

\sum_jg_j\Psi_j=1, \qquad\Psi(0)=\frac{e_0}{6c_0^5}.

Let D_t,D_{c_2},D_{p_0},D_{v_0} be the time derivative and the three endpoint derivations, uniquely extended through the finite separable coefficient field. They commute and preserve E[t]. For

M=(\Psi,D_t\Phi,D_{c_2}\Phi,D_{p_0}\Phi,D_{v_0}\Phi), \qquad J=\det M,

the anchored endpoint gives

J(0)=\frac1{6c_0^5}\ne0.

This checks the evaluation determinant, not merely the incidence Jacobian. The former is needed for the differential-form construction.

There is an actual embedding

L=\operatorname{Frac}(k[X_0,X_1,X_2,p,v]/K)\hookrightarrow E(t)

given by X_j\mapsto\Phi_j. Independence of the endpoint parameters, together with the exact coordinate \Phi_1=t, proves injectivity on the four remaining coordinates. Monicity and irreducibility of K give the hypersurface-field embedding. Both fields have transcendence degree four over k, and the source is finitely generated, so this extension is finite. These are proved in BundleConicEmbedding.lean; dominance is not assumed.

4. An effective primitive

Set \beta=\rho(\Phi), b_j=\rho_j(\Phi), and H_{jk}=K_{jk}(\Phi). Define the polynomial

P=J\sum_j\Psi_jb_j+ \beta\left(\sum_{\alpha=0}^3 \det(M[\alpha+1\leftarrow D_\alpha\Psi]) -J\sum_{j,k}\Psi_jH_{jk}\Psi_k\right)\in E[t].

Here D_0=D_t, and replacing column \alpha+1 uses the normal column as column zero. Integrate coefficientwise:

I(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{[t^n]P}{n+1}t^{n+1}.

This sum is finite. For the four tail coordinates, put

w_j=\beta\Psi_j-\frac{I}{J}D_t\Phi_j \quad(j=X_1,X_2,p,v).

The shared polynomial-family identity, with its actual hypotheses discharged here, gives

\operatorname{resDiv}_{K_0}(w)=-r_\rho, \quad r_\rho=\frac{\rho_0}{K_0}-\frac{\rho K_{00}}{K_0^2} =\frac{p(4X_0^3-X_1p)}{18\lambda(2X_0^3+X_1p)^2}.

Here, for j\in\{X_1,X_2,p,v\}, the tangent derivation is \delta_j=\partial_j-(K_j/K_0)\partial_0, and the precise divergence is

\operatorname{resDiv}_{K_0}(w) =\sum_j\delta_j(w_j)-\frac{\sum_jw_j\delta_j(K_0)}{K_0} =K_0\sum_j\delta_j\!\left(\frac{w_j}{K_0}\right).

The normalized finite field trace from E(t) to L preserves this equation. BundleQuotientPrimitive.lean proves the resulting existence in the actual quotient field. Its theorem works for any polynomial density over \mathbb C(\lambda); the final application is exactly \rho=-X_0^3p/(6\lambda).

5. Physical pullback and legal denominators

The physical field embedding is constructed independently. Let h=Y_0^3+iY_2^3. In the sextic field,

Y_1^6=\lambda(p^3+iv^3),\qquad Y_0=p/Y_1,\qquad Y_2=v/Y_1.

The remaining variable X_0 satisfies a monic equation. Hence the physical field is algebraic over the field generated by (\lambda,X_1,X_2,p,v). Its transcendence degree is five, so those five parameters are algebraically independent. This proves injectivity before localizing rational-function denominators. The monic quotient model then gives the actual embedding of L into the sextic field.

BundlePhysicalTransport.lean checks the full five-tail Jacobian and its inverse, including the \lambda coordinate, all derivative compatibilities, its determinant -6\lambda Y_1, and the normal-source identity. The quotient vector has zero \lambda component; its other components may depend on \lambda. No physical derivative is discarded by treating the parameter as a constant.

Choose legal rational lifts of the traced tail components and complete them by

W_0=\frac{\rho-\sum_{j\ne0}K_jW_j}{K_0},\qquad W_\lambda=0.

The normal identity is then W(K)=\rho, and the proved residue equation says \operatorname{div}W\in(K) in the legal localization. The full Piola transform is

V=J_\theta(d\theta)^{-1}(W\circ\theta).

In the following component formulas each W_j is evaluated at \theta, and \lambda=Y_1^3/h. For example, writing \Lambda=Y_0^2W_p+iY_2^2W_v, its three Y-components are

\begin{aligned} V_{Y_0}&=-6\lambda W_p+3\lambda Y_0\Lambda/h,\\ V_{Y_1}&=-3\lambda Y_1\Lambda/h,\\ V_{Y_2}&=-6\lambda W_v+3\lambda Y_2\Lambda/h, \end{aligned}

and V_{X_j}=-6\lambda Y_1W_{X_j}. The residue implementation uses the equivalent five-tail Piola identity and normal-source binding. It retains all six components when passing to the certificate.

The chart denominators, normal denominator, and traced coefficients are nonzero in the actual sextic function field. The trusted residue clearing and finite homogeneous extraction theorems therefore produce some finite d, a nonzero homogeneous D coprime to F over \mathbb C, six homogeneous N_j of degree d+1, and Q with

A_*D^2-D\sum_jN_jF_j+ F\left(D\sum_j\partial_jN_j-\sum_jN_j\partial_jD\right)=F^2Q.

The final theorem has no field-map, conic, primitive, normal, or determinant hypothesis. This route does not require Q=0.
