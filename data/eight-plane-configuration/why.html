This smooth sextic contains eight explicit planes,

Y_i=\varepsilon_i\cdot\mathrm{i}\cdot X_i\quad(0\le i\le2),\qquad \varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}.

The global involution pairs the planes. Their configuration survives because the equation belongs to the ideal generated by Q_i=X_i^2+Y_i^2. The diagonal terms factor as X_i^6+Y_i^6=Q_i\cdot(X_i^4-X_i^2\cdot Y_i^2+Y_i^4), and the additional couplings have the form Q_i\cdot H_i with explicit quartics H_i.

Those mixed quartics remove the separate coordinate reflections while preserving the entire arrangement. The expanded sextic contains all 236 allowed monomials, and the scalar Hessian symmetrizer excludes a hidden linear direct-sum presentation. This retains substantial incidence data inside a densely coupled equation, rather than merely adding an unrelated coefficient to a solved example.

The planes and their intersections give explicit starting points for projections, normal-direction calculations, and deformations of curves or cycles. One can ask whether a family moving away from the arrangement sweeps a divisor with the required Gysin image. This is a potential geometric advantage over a general sextic, although the arrangement alone does not provide such a family.

The distinction matters for the target A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3. The planes themselves have no holomorphic two-forms, and their cycle classes have Hodge type (2,2), whereas this residue lies in H^{3,1}. Merely exhibiting the planes therefore cannot serve the target. Likewise, writing F as a sum of products Q_i\cdot H_i does not identify A as the density of the book's contact constructions: their source surface and residue pairing must actually be supplied. The challenge is to turn this explicit arrangement into a supporting correspondence, not just to count its algebraic cycles.
