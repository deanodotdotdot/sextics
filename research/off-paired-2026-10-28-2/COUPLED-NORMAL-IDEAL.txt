COUPLED CRITICAL-NORMAL IDEAL: CHARACTERISTIC-ZERO UNIT-IDEAL PROOF
Agent-only computational research. This is a scalar critical-algebra result,
not an eight-row current, an original certificate, or a solved sextic.

Let u=j^2 and work over Q in i,u,w,x. Define
 T=1+w^3, N=9i^2w^2-16uT, L=256T^5N+729i^6u w^6,
 AV=64u w T^2N, BV=9i^2L, vp=AV/BV, B=i*u*w*T*N*L,
 Q=8(i x^2+2x+3)^2-u x^2(2i x^2+5x+8),
 H=AV(2i x^2+5x+8)(x+2)-4BV x^6.
Define the three polynomial normal generators (subscripts mean derivatives):
 mi=2iNL x^2-(i N_i L-2NL-i L_i N)(x+2),
 mj=-4NL(i x^2+2x+3)-(2NL+2u N_u L-2u L_u N)(x+2),
 mw=-(NLT+2w T_w NL+w N_w LT-w L_w NT)(x+2).

Claim: the ideal I=(Q,H,mi,mj,mw) becomes the unit ideal in
 Q[i,u,w,x,1/B]. Equivalently, B^m belongs to I for some finite m.
The argument below proves this over characteristic zero. It does NOT give
an explicit exponent or rational multipliers. A separate calculation proves
B itself is in the corresponding ideal modulo1009; that finite-field fact
is not substituted for the characteristic-zero argument.

Proof.
Suppose there were a complex common zero with B nonzero. Since Q(0)=72,
x is nonzero. Choose j with j^2=u and set
 a=-2(i x^2+2x+3)/(j x), A2=i x^2+5x/2+4.
The exact identity a^2-A2=Q/(2j^2 x^2) gives a^2=A2. Since H=0,
 vp*A2*(x+2)=2x^6.
All factors on the right and vp are nonzero. Hence a and x+2 are nonzero.
Set y=x^3/a and t=y^2/vp=(x+2)/2. Then
 ay=x^3,
 C=y^2/vp+a^2+i x^2+j a x+x+1=0.
At fixed base parameters, regard y=x^3/a. Direct substitution gives
 a*C_a=2a^2+j a x-2t=0,
 x*C_x=2i x^2+j a x+x+6t=0.
The three remaining derivatives at fixed x,a are exactly
 i*C_i=mi/(2NL), j*C_j=mj/(2NL), w*C_w=mw/(2NLT).
Consequently C and all five derivatives of C in (i,j,w,x,a) vanish.

This is impossible because this chart is an etale finite cover of an open
subset of the original smooth affine sextic. Here are explicit inverse
choices and a Jacobian check, so the assertion does not rely on a picture
or on a generic-point-only smoothness inference.
Choose Z with Z^2=w, put k=3iw/(4T), and put
 R=k^2*T/j^2-1=N/(16uT).
Choose X with X^3=a*j/k; choose U with U^6=R*X^6; then set
 Y=2T*x/(Z*X), P=k*X*Y/U.
Every coordinate and every chosen root is nonzero. The definitions recover
 k=UP/(XY), R=(U/X)^6, i=4kT/(3w), j^2=k^2T/(1+R),
 x=ZXY/(2T), a=kX^3/j, y=Z^3Y^3j/(8kT^3).
Also 1+R=k^2T/j^2 is nonzero. With S=k^6/R, L nonzero implies
1+S nonzero. The physical v expression agrees with
 w^3/[64T^4(1+R)(1+S)].
Before imposing any hypersurface equation, the exact identity is
 C=f/T,
 f=1+X^6+U^6+Y^6+Z^6+P^6+XYZ/2+X^3UZP/2+XUYP/3.
The logarithmic Jacobian of (i,j,w,x,a) with respect to (X,U,Y,Z,P)
is -18R/(1+R), with the stated ordering. Thus its ordinary Jacobian is
this nonzero scalar times i*j*w*x*a/(X*U*Y*Z*P). The root choices are etale
on this open torus. Therefore dC=0 on C=0 would imply df=0 on f=0.

The homogeneous original F is smooth over C: the coefficient L1 bounds
of its perturbation gradients are (13/6,7/3,5/6,5/6,1,5/6), all less
than6. At a nonzero complex vector, choose a coordinate of maximal modulus
M. Its Fermat derivative has modulus6M^5, whereas all perturbation terms
in that derivative have total modulus at most cM^5 with c<6. Hence the
homogeneous gradient cannot vanish. If df vanished on f=0, Euler's
homogeneous identity would also make the omitted X0 derivative vanish.
This is the required contradiction.

The localized ideal therefore has no complex zero. The Nullstellensatz
makes it the unit ideal over C; faithful field extension descends the
unit-ideal statement to Q. Clearing powers of B proves the claim.

What this does and does not resolve.
This removes a common-zero obstruction to coupling the physical normal
directions on the indicated critical chart. Scalar Bezout coefficients
exist using only the listed chart denominators. It does not export those
coefficients, convert them into the compact eight-row connection basis,
serve the chart boundary or infinity, lower the full actual residual, or
verify the required original F^2 identity. The frozen Taylor current with
nonzero residual and only Delta^3 denominator remains the preferred actual
current. The scalar unit-ideal result must not be presented as a certificate.
